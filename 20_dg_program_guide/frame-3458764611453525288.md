### Image 2 — data domain reference card (six panels, teal/orange)
**Definition** — "A data domain is a logical grouping of related data elements that share common characteristics. It provides structure and context for data management, ensuring consistency, quality, and usability across an organization."

**Why to define data domains** — *Purpose:* "Separate and organize data elements into distinct areas of ownership and responsibility, improving manageability, and governance while minimizing the impact of changes on other domains". *Problem solved:* "Inefficient data management, lack of clear ownership, and conflicting data strategies, distinct areas of responsibility not defined".

**Terms & definitions**
- Data Domain: a logical grouping of related data elements within one area of responsibility
- Data Governance: policies and processes for managing data domains and ensuring its data quality
- Data Product: a valuable group of data assets published from a data domain to support a use case
- Data Contract: agreement defining data structure, quality, and usage

**Data domain examples**
- Customer: contact information, demographics, preferences, interactions
- Product: specifications, inventory, pricing, sales history
- Finance: transactions, accounts, budgets, forecasts
- Human Resources: employee records, compensation, performance reviews, benefits

**Owner roles & responsibilities**
- Data Domain Owner: responsible for managing a data domain within its business context
- Data Product Owner: responsible for a data product's success, promotion and day-to-day operations
- Data Steward: ensures data quality and compliance within a domain by validating the data quality status of all data assets within the domain

**Business roles**
- Subject Matter Experts: provide domain-specific knowledge and expertise
- Business Sponsors: fund and prioritize data domain initiatives
- Executives: align data domains with strategic objectives and measure their impact on business outcomes

**Technology roles**
- Data Architect: designs and implements data models, ensuring alignment with business needs
- Data Engineer: builds and maintains data pipelines
- Data Analyst: explores and analyzes data to derive insights
- Data Scientist: develops and deploys machine learning models to automate tasks and predict outcomes
