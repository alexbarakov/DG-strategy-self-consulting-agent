## Summary
The master frame of the guide: "Data / AI Governance Program Map 3.0" by Alex Barakov (Data Nature). It stages the whole DG program from concept to implementation: 0. Basic Concepts of DG → 1. Determining whether a DG program is necessary → 2. Building a DG framework, goals and stakeholder buy-in (with sub-tracks 2.1 "DG with Common Sense" and 2.2 DG MVP) → 3. Launching the DG program → 4. Launching specific projects/initiatives from the most critical streams (4.3 data catalog, 4.4 data certification/data products, 4.5 data quality management, 4.6 data operations, 4.7 privacy compliance and security, 4.8 data literacy) → 5. Development of the DG framework (5.1 operating model, 5.2 completing the DG solution/framework, 5.3 cost-benefit model, 5.4 further initiatives). Each stage contains 100+ hexagon topics; explicit "fail" branches describe why programs stall. Includes author's essays ("My thoughts", "DG Kitchen of the Tech Industry", "Manifesto of an effective data governance leader"), definitions, ROI/value framing, role descriptions, and a large curated resource library (books, blogs, articles).

## Objects
- **Intro blocks:** What is this? (guide purpose — organize DG practices without hating the subject); How to Read/Use it (stages with hexagon topics; use as guide or as template for your own DG map); For Whom (data leaders, CDOs, analysts/engineers as stewards/custodians, business function management); Disclaimer
- **Definitions:** Data Governance as cross-functional program managing data as a corporate asset (Villar & Kushner); DG as auditor analogy (DAMA-DMBOK); DAMA and DMBOK2 described (independent, "100% theoretical and largely impractical"); Data Quality definitions (academic vs Villar & Kushner actionable version); data stewardship definition and steward/custodian distinction
- **Stage 1 — is DG necessary:** analysis of company readiness / D&A maturity; formalization of business value drivers (top-down); identification of pain points of business and data teams (bottom-up); identification of major barriers and risks; DG Drivers 1-3 (confidentiality risk/access focus; regulatory compliance risk/reporting accuracy; process accuracy-speed/cost reduction); fail branches: "driver not found / blocking obstacles / D&A maturity low"; value drivers insufficient for stakeholder buy-in
- **Stage 2 — framework and buy-in:** DG Vision; assembling a custom DG solution/framework (realistic, prioritized set of components); financial ROI model; defining the governance leader (must be an "esteemed veteran" — external hires lack authority, sabotage risk); formalization of program goals; 2.1 Common Sense DG (clear-value team-level projects); 2.2 DG MVP within one "mature" domain (also good for testing ideas and accelerating benefits, not just for approval problems); leadership essay: pointless to expect enthusiastic business support — data/analytics leaders must arrive at DG themselves
- **Stage 3 — launch:** DG program launch agreed and approved; adoption of company DG policy (regulations, normative docs); establishment of a Data Office; detailed roadmap; clarification and distribution of roles; project plan for 1/2/3-year horizons; communication and marketing system (portal, newsletters); linking DG with other D&A projects (SS BI, SS ETL, Data Mesh) and strategic initiatives; creation of additional roles/management bodies as adoption progresses; fail branch: overestimating oneself — insufficient determination to overcome resistance and passivity; "pseudo DG" risk — only data catalog implemented, no business involvement
- **Stage 4 — critical stream projects:**
  - Data catalog (4.3): goals/stakeholders/budget; requirements; tool selection; metadata integration from DWH/DL and sources; lineage for ETL packages; BI report layer integration; business glossary creation and linkage; domain structure and labeling automation; criticality scoring methodology; DQ status in object card and lineage; metadata completion work; documentation-as-code in lifecycle CI/CD; MVP catalog to production with data power users; ownership/stewardship role model in catalog; limited functionality for casual users; advanced column-level lineage; source schemas and data contracts representation; certification reflection; PII classification automation; catalog adoption analysis; active metadata lake
  - Data certification / Data products (4.4): certification criteria (quality, completeness, relevance, security, documentation, ownership); certification workflow (business requirements, data audit, label); automation in catalog; transparency (status in lineage/profiles); certification monitoring dashboard; certified data products; domain team training on owning data products; data product framework; MDM integration; priority business cases; testing and launch coordination
  - Data quality management (4.5): DQMS scope definition; regulatory/organizational frameworks; operational DQ processes (profiling, cleansing, assessment, monitoring); DQM tools deployment; metrics/checks library; DQ in pipelines; DQ statuses in catalog; global DQ checks via metadata at DWH/DL level; one-off DQ assessment; critical-data identification logic automation; project work on improving critical data quality per domain; from DQ monitoring to data observability
  - Data operations (4.6): CI/CD for data; ETL/ELT orchestration automation; automated data testing; infrastructure organization (microservices, containerization); monitoring and observability; logging/alerts; performance monitoring; team interaction organization; version control; participation in catalog and DQ tooling deployment
  - Privacy/security (4.7): analysis of regulatory/industry standards per location; initial compliance audit; PII classification automation; right-to-know / right-to-delete solutions; role-based access restriction; role-based access matrix + IDM RLS; privacy office; risk identification and vulnerability scanning; leakage prevention; data minimization; security trainings incl. phishing; periodic audits; data handling ethics research
  - Data literacy (4.8): hiring data-literate employees; D&A competency matrix for key roles; modern learning system (onboarding, mandatory/group training, marathons, skill belts, community gamification)
  - Data stewardship program: defining steward role, typology, boundaries; identification and training; integration into DG/DQ projects; automation of steward workplace in catalog, regular domain audits; steward/custodian dashboards; scaling stewardship into a data culture program, Data Stewards Forum
- **Stage 5 — framework development:** operational model for managing the program; refinement of process scopes; regulatory documentation support system; financial efficiency assessment; DG maturity assessment; DG metrics and scorecards; operational monitoring tools; sober roadmap refinement (abandon ineffective practices, capacity analysis); transition to active non-invasive DG (embed in tools, shift from post-reaction to daily workflows, metadata chatbots); data monetization / data cost management; data marketplace migration; knowledge graph management; shift left; gamification of DG; community-led decentralized DG; data risk management; data strategy ("DG is just one of many topics within data strategy"); implementation of data contracts with backend systems and DWH/DL teams; MDM implementation; CDO Office; DG business cases
- **Author's commentary:** "My thoughts" / "Data Governance Kitchen of the Tech Industry": efficiency estimations are mostly emotional, nobody has comprehensive DG effectiveness analysis; DG often conflicts with tech-company culture (regulations and rituals dismissed as nonsense, teams stop halfway); role adoption doesn't happen; only 1 in 20 companies has a separate AI Governance initiative; most leading companies live in "common-sense DG" chaos and "don't break"; the data platform team "hides" behind the data catalog; Data Custodian / Data Partner / Domain Curator likely more important than Data Steward. Manifesto of an effective DG leader: truly believe in DG; implement practically not "in outer space"; concisely explain DG; never start without IT and business stakeholder support. Closing advice: DG and DQM are ongoing investments; stage order is a generalization — improvise; return to your simplified framework when overwhelmed; embrace decentralized community-led DG
- **Data cleaning misconception block:** businesses believe all data must be cleaned first — McKinsey: companies waste up to 70% of effort on cleaning; focus cleaning on critical use cases while developing corporate ontology and core data model; cautionary example of a company spending hundreds of millions on a unified data metamodel in vain
- **Roles:** Data Domain-level roles — domain owner responsibilities (definition, policy, quality, security, relationships; compliance; working sessions on change requests; confidentiality levels) and requirements (organized, team builder, authority, communicator, change agent, consensus builder, empowered; broad business perspective, data analysis/modeling experience)
- **Resources:** books list (Seiner, Ladley, Madsen, Eryurek, Sen, Templar, Plotkin, Soares, Reichental, DMBOK2, Ledoux ebook); personal blogs (Nicola Askham, George Firican, Dave Wells, Charlotte Ledoux, Robert Seiner, Animesh Kumar, Piotr Czarnas, Chad Sanderson, Willem Koenders, Peter van Nederpelt, Irina Steenbeek); article cards per stage; templates gallery; author footer (Alex Barakov, The Hague; data-nature.com, t.me/datanature, LinkedIn, Calendly coaching); links to other boards (Lords of the Boards, BI Adoption Boost Guide, Data & Analytics Maturity Canvas) and Excel template of the guide
