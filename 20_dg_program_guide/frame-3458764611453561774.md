## Summary
Four labelled columns read left to right: **DG Value drivers** → **Data Governace Goals** [sic] → **DG Initiatives** → **Most common Success factors**, wired together with ~110 connectors. Instruction text under the title: "Define the goals that are most important for your company. Remove goals that are not relevant. Check the linked DG streams that will help you achieve these goals and identify success factors. Remove the rest." Credited "Data Nature".

## Objects

### Column 1 — DG Value drivers (5 stickies)
Increasing the speed of launching and developing new business products and services; Reducing the time-to-market for data analytics projects; Increasing trust in data and data products; Increasing the overall utility of data and analytics for the business; Reducing costs.

### Column 2 — Data Governance Goals (11 stickies, top to bottom)
Reduce lead time for data discovery operations by analysts and engineers; Achieve desired and stable levels of quality for the most important data; Make the data quality status transparent for the most important data objects; Increase the reuse of data objects and reporting capabilities; Eliminate inconsistencies in the interpretation and values of business metrics; Minimize duplicate efforts in data preparation and cleaning for business tasks; Speed up the hiring process and onboarding for newcomers in data roles, reduce turnover within the data team; Accelerate the integration of acquired companies; Optimize resources for storing and maintaining data warehouse objects; Reduce compliance and security risks; Increase the quantity, quality, and depth of penetration of data/analytics products into the business.

### Value driver → Goal wiring (as drawn)
- *Increasing the speed of launching new business products and services* → reduce lead time for data discovery; increase reuse of data objects; minimize duplicate efforts; achieve stable quality for the most important data; accelerate integration of acquired companies.
- *Reducing the time-to-market for data analytics projects* → minimize duplicate efforts; achieve stable quality; eliminate metric inconsistencies; reduce lead time for data discovery.
- *Increasing trust in data and data products* → make DQ status transparent; eliminate metric inconsistencies.
- *Increasing the overall utility of data and analytics for the business* → increase penetration of data/analytics products; minimize duplicate efforts; increase reuse of data objects; achieve stable quality.
- *Reducing costs* → optimize storage/maintenance resources; minimize duplicate efforts; speed up hiring and onboarding; reduce compliance and security risks.

### Column 3 — DG Initiatives: 10 streams (rounded shapes) with their sticky sub-initiatives
Grouping below follows the vertical bands on the board.
1. **Roles model and operating framework** — Data ownership; Data stawardship [sic] and custodianship; DG/CDO office, DG Committee etc; Service SLAs.
2. **Data quality management system** — Predictable and rapid resolution of data quality tasks for critical objects; Effective notification of data consumers about the status and issues in data quality; Data quality checks coverage and processing flow; "…".
3. **Metadata management** — Data cataloging; Business Glossary; Lineage; Documentation; Business ontology; "…".
4. **Building the golden layer** — MDM; data products; Layer of trusted certified data sources; "…".
5. **Data Classification** — Data Domain structure; Sensitivity / PII detection and labeling; Criticality labeling; "…".
6. **Data standards** — Data lifecycle standards as a code; Data platform medallion layers standard; DWH Naming convention; Data Architecture Standards.
7. **Analytics Governance** — BI Content certification and health assessment; BI Ops; Metric store and Unified semantic layer as a code; Centralized and Self-service content transition flow.
8. **Data Platform Development** — Standardized operations in the data lifecycle on the data platform; DevOps; DWH School and standardized hiring; Change release Impact assessment; Unified data tooling (Golden Path); Data contracts.
9. **Data Culture Development** — Data Culture Development; Data Literacy projects; "…".
10. **Data security and Compliance** — Automated access management and approval flows; Access audit synced with PII classification; Role-based Data Access Metrix [sic]; Personal and critical data are labeled and managed within specialized processes.
