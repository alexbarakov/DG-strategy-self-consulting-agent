## Summary
A hub-and-spoke frame. A large hexagon in the middle carries the definition of a DG framework; ten dotted arrows radiate from it to seven published frameworks, one analogy, the "how to create your own" recipe and the board's own framework template. Definition text on the hexagon: the DG framework is "a specific simplified structure that guides the implementation of Data Governance in an organization"; per the **Data Governance Institute** it is *"A logical structure for classifying, organizing, and communicating the complex activities associated with enterprise data decision-making and action-taking."* In other words it is *"the how — a blueprint for enforcing governance"*, the conceptual foundation of a DG program that must visualise how quality, integrity, security, accessibility and usability of data assets are ensured.

The framework diagrams themselves are images; their contents are reconstructed below.

## Objects

### DGI Data Governance Framework (image)
Board's own note: ten universal components covering why-what-who-how, split into three core areas — **rules, people, processes**. Highlighted components: *Goals, Metrics and Funding* (a program that increases revenue, optimizes costs, ensures business sustainability despite risks and disruptions); *Controls* (risk management, preventive or corrective, applied at various framework levels); *Data Governance Office (DGO)* (oversees the program, interacts with stakeholders, aligns data policies and standards, maintains program documentation).
The diagram itself lays the ten components out on three bands. **Rules & Rules of Engagement** — *Why:* (1) Mission → "To Achieve"; *What:* (2) Focus Areas → Goals / Metrics-Success Measures / Funding; (3) Data Rules and Definitions; (4) Decision Rights; (5) Accountabilities; (6) Control Mechanisms. **People & Organizational Bodies** — *Who:* (7) Data Stakeholders, (8) Data Governance Offices (DGO), (9) Data Stewards. **Processes** — *When / How:* (10) Data Governance Processes, a 7-step chevron: 1 Develop a value statement → 2 Prepare a Roadmap → 3 Plan and Fund → 4 Design the Program → 5 Deploy the Program → 6 Govern the Data → 7 Monitor, Measure, Report; all feeding "Business / IT Processes That Touch Data" under the label **Management**.
Footer panel of the image — *Definition:* "Data Governance is the exercise of decision making and authority for data-related matters. It's a system of decision rights and accountabilities for information-related processes, executed according to agreed upon models which describe who can take what actions with what information and under what circumstances, using what methods." *Processes for governing how data is used, and when, and by whom* (12): 1 Aligning Policies, Requirements & Controls; 2 Establishing Decision Rights; 3 Establishing Accountability; 4 Performing Stewardship; 5 Managing Change; 6 Defining Data; 7 Issue Resolution; 8 Specifying Data Quality Requirements; 9 Building Governance into Technology; 10 Stakeholder Care and Support; 11 Stakeholder Communications; 12 Measuring and Reporting Value.

### Informatica's Data Governance Framework (image)
Header: "Measure and Monitor (Business and Technology KPIs)". Five stacked bands, each with four examples, chained by the left-hand connectors *Supported by → Driven by → Empowered by → Enabled by*:
- **Strategic Business Drivers** — business goals of the organization. Examples: Loyalty, Compliance, Profitability, Lower Costs.
- **Governance Outcomes** — data conditions needed to support the business goals. Examples: Customer 360, Lineage Clarity, Quality Data, Standards.
- **People** — creating a data-enabled organization culture. Examples: DG Org, Roles, Communication, Training.
- **Processes** — the way we work to understand and improve data. Examples: Define Rules, Set Standards, Manage Issues, Onboard Data.
- **Technology** — intelligent solution capabilities to automate and scale. Examples: Metadata, Data Catalog, Master Data, Data Quality.

### How to create your own data governance framework? (text)
1. Study several existing frameworks. 2. Draft what should definitely be the focus: there are **core elements**; there are issues that are **more pressing specifically for you**; there are things that are better to **exclude for now** to avoid distractions. 3. Sit down with your team for half a day and **"sketch it out"**. Caveat under the recipe: "This is not yet a data governance implementation project; it's an abstract model of how it will work." An arrow points on to the board's own **Data Governance framework template**.
