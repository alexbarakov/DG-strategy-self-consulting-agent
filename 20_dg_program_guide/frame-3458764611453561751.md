## Summary
Overview of well-known Data Governance framework models and guidance on building your own. Defines a DG framework (per the Data Governance Institute) as "a logical structure for classifying, organizing, and communicating the complex activities associated with enterprise data decision-making and action-taking" — i.e. "the how, a blueprint for enforcing governance", the conceptual foundation of a DG program covering quality, integrity, security, accessibility and usability of data assets. A central hexagon links out to seven reference frameworks plus a practical recipe for drafting your own. 21 images (framework diagrams) were not machine-readable; open the deep-link for visuals.

## Objects
- Central hexagon "Data governance framework" with definition text (DGI quote) and links to all framework cards
- **DGI Data Governance Framework** — ten universal components (why-what-who-how); highlighted: Goals/Metrics/Funding, Controls (preventive/corrective), Data Governance Office (DGO); components grouped into rules, people, processes
- **DAMA Wheel** — knowledge areas of data management with Data Governance at the center (Strategy, Organization and Roles, Policies and Standards, Case Management, Value Assessment); other areas (architecture, modeling, storage) positioned around it
- **DAMA Pyramid (Peter Aiken)** — incorporates DMBOK2 knowledge areas, shows the stage a company is at and how to progress toward robust data management
- **The Eckerson Group DG Framework** — six layers, 39 components, people-centric: "we don't govern the data, we govern what people do when working with data"
- **Informatica's Data Governance Framework**
- **Non-Invasive Data Governance (Robert S. Seiner)**
- **PwC enterprise data governance framework**
- **"Just nice analogy" from Willem Koenders**
- **How to create your own DG framework** — 1) study several existing frameworks; 2) draft the focus (core elements; issues most pressing for you; things to exclude for now); 3) sit down with the team for half a day and "sketch it out". Note: this is not yet an implementation project, it's an abstract model of how it will work
