## Summary
Author's concluding insights on what makes DG succeed regardless of "heavy" or "light" implementation. Key theses: cross-role initiatives need genuine team buy-in; explaining DG to the business yields only superficial support — DG must be driven by data/analytics leaders and depends on team maturity in key positions; the MVP DG tactic is valuable for testing ideas and accelerating benefits; the Core BI / cross-functional analytics team is critical (metrics tree, glossary, object classification, golden layer, content management, data curator for weak domains); the Data Custodian/Partner/Curator role is more foundational than Data Steward; and the DG leader must be a "respected veteran," preferably internal — external hires face credibility gaps leading to sabotage and token efforts.

## Objects
- Team alignment: genuine buy-in required for cross-role initiatives
- Business expectation management: superficial support; data/analytics leaders must drive
- MVP DG tactic: idea testing, foundation building, faster benefit realization
- Core BI / cross-functional analytics team as DG backbone
- Data Custodian / Data Partner / Data Curator > Data Steward in foundational importance
- DG leader profile: respected internal veteran; communication, authority, resilience, willpower
