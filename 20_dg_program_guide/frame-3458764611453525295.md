## Objects

### Executive Sponsor (text block)
Who is this: usually a business leader with significant organizational influence, often the head of a business function. Responsibilities: holds the highest responsibility for the data domain (operational decisions may be delegated to data stewards, but ultimate accountability rests with them); oversees data definitions, policies and their implementation at a high level; responsible for compliance with quality, accuracy, relevance and audit requirements; approves all policies for the domain; acts as arbitrator in issues between data domains; conducts working sessions to resolve issues that violate established policies.

### Data custodian (text block)
An IT role performed by people who manage the technical environment for data maintenance, movement and storage — DBAs, data modelers, ETL developers; often carried out by domain system analysts, BI professionals or data engineers. Example responsibilities: managing and controlling data access; ensuring data integrity through technical processes; executing technical processes to resolve DQ issues together with the Data Steward; protecting data using technical controls; verifying alignment of new data with the common data model sets; maintaining master data versions with change history; applying change management to database maintenance; verifying data content and changes. Closing remark (highlighted green): "Recognizable, isn't it? This is the typical day-to-day work of our teams, without any extra roles."

### Diagram — DATA OWNER (image next to the "Data owner" label)
Caption: "A business leader who is responsible for keeping data correct, safe, and properly used in their part of the organization." Orange centre node DATA OWNER with four teal lobes, each expanding into two detail cards:
- **Business Accountability** → *Sets strategic direction* (defines data quality targets and KPIs; establishes business rules for data usage; allocates resources for data management) and *Approves governance policies* (reviews and approves data access requests; signs off on data sharing agreements; validates data classification levels).
- **Technical Oversight** → *Defines quality requirements* (establishes DQ thresholds and expectations; approves data validation rules; reviews quality monitoring reports and incidents) and *Manages data architecture* (approves changes to data models and architecture; signs off on data integrations to other systems; validates data lifecycle rules).
- **Cross-functional Leadership** → *Partners with IT* (aligns technical solutions with business needs; reviews system change impacts to the business model; approves technical implementations) and *Directs data stewardship* (delegates operational responsibilities; reviews stewardship goals and effectiveness; ensures proper data maintenance procedures).
- **Risk & Compliance** → *Manages data risks* (identifies potential data risks; reviews, approves and manages mitigation strategies; watches for signs of data problems and anomalies) and *Ensures compliance* (checks which rules apply to the data and processes; answers to auditors in a regulatory audit; keeps required proof of following data rules).

### Diagram — Business Data Steward vs Technical Data Steward (image, credited to DQOps, https://dqops.com/)
Subtitle: a data steward is a guardian of an organization's data, ensuring its accuracy, quality and appropriate use; the business/technical distinction integrates strategic goals with technical implementation. Centre chain: Business Data Steward — Data Steward (orange) — Technical Data Steward, under the heading "Data Quality Responsibilities of Data Stewards" and the board author's own note "We should separate the roles of business and technical data stewards".
- Business Data Steward (12 items): categorize data assets by importance; identify Critical Data Assets for DQ control; define data quality requirements; ensure all relevant data assets are profiled; define business data rules; define data quality standards; educate business users on data policies and standards; assess and resolve DQ issues from a business impact standpoint; review and analyze data profiling and quality reports; manage data dictionaries and business glossaries; participate in data governance councils; collaborate with IT on DQ implementation.
- Technical Data Steward (12 items): translate business rules into technical requirements; advise on technical feasibility of DG initiatives; integrate data profiling tools; configure data observability tools and integrate them into data pipelines; profile and analyze data to identify DQ trends; configure and maintain requested DQ checks; define reusable DQ checks to validate standards; collaborate with technical data stewards of upstream and downstream systems; configure DQ incident management workflows; review new DQ issues before forwarding them to business data stewards; develop DQ dashboards and reports; fix DQ problems directly in the data source.

### Diagram — Data producer (image under the DG Office column)
Caption: "A data producer is the platform or team that creates, manages, and makes data available to others for use." Three stacked layers, each with three cards, connected top-down by dashed arrows (teal → orange → teal):
- **Data Ownership**: *Data Ownership* (producers take responsibility for their data, are the primary point of contact and ensure it is delivered on time and in good condition); *Promote Data Product* (promote the product internally and demonstrate the data's value for the business); *Data Evolution* (adapt to evolving data needs and technologies — updating formats, adopting new solutions, evolving governance practices).
- **Data Contract** (orange-outlined cards): *Data Security and Privacy* (security and privacy of the data, especially sensitive/PII); *Data Quality* (data accurate and reliable, monitor quality, fix problems, keep data users informed); *Data Schema Stability* (schema remains stable; changes impact users, so modifications go through a formal process).
- **Data Sharing**: *Data Documentation* (documentation of structure and meaning, published in a data catalog or through data contracts); *Easy Data Consumption* (clear, user-friendly access — well-documented interfaces and descriptions of schema and access methods); *Data Governance* (clear policies outlining roles and responsibilities for data management and defining DQ standards).
Bottom row: four consumer icons (people / analytics / database / AI-brain) fed by dashed arrows from the Data Sharing layer.
